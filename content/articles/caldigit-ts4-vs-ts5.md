The CalDigit TS4 and TS5 are the same size, weigh the same 1.41 lb, and sit at nearly the same launch price. They are not the same dock. The TS4 is the Thunderbolt 4 model with **more USB ports and a DisplayPort output**; the TS5 is the Thunderbolt 5 model with **140W charging and three downstream Thunderbolt 5 ports**, but no DisplayPort and three fewer USB ports.

That makes this a rare comparison where "newer" does not automatically mean "better for you". The right answer depends on your Mac's Thunderbolt generation, your monitors, and whether you need 140W.

We did not test either dock. Specifications come from CalDigit's TS4 and TS5 pages, checked on 8 November 2026; launch prices come from The Verge's 2022 and 2025 announcements and are labelled as launch prices.

## Quick answer

- **Buy the TS4** if you have a Thunderbolt 4 Mac, two displays, a DisplayPort monitor, and a pile of USB-A accessories. It is the better port fit, and its 98W charging is enough for a MacBook Air or a 14-inch MacBook Pro.
- **Buy the TS5** if you have a Thunderbolt 5 Mac (M5 Pro or M5 Max), need three or four displays, want 140W for a 16-inch MacBook Pro, or plan to upgrade the laptop within the dock's life.
- **Buy neither** if you need one display and a few accessories. A USB-C hub is the correct spend.

## What is the same

The shared parts are why this is a real decision:

- **2.5GbE Ethernet** on both, with the same caveat that Ethernet requires a Thunderbolt or PCIe-capable USB4 host.
- **SD and microSD 4.0 UHS-II** card readers.
- **Three 3.5mm audio ports**: front combo, rear output, rear input.
- **Dual 6K60 displays** on a Thunderbolt 4 host, and a single 8K60 display.
- **15W downstream Thunderbolt power**.
- **The same chassis footprint and 1.41 lb weight.** The TS5 is not a bigger desk object; it is a different port layout in the same box.

If those are the features you need, the TS4 does them for less money at launch, and the TS5 does them with newer Thunderbolt ports.

## The five differences that matter

### 1. Thunderbolt 4 versus Thunderbolt 5

The TS4's three Thunderbolt ports run at **40Gb/s**. The TS5's Thunderbolt 5 link runs at **80Gb/s**, or up to **120Gb/s** in Bandwidth Boost mode for video, according to CalDigit.

For a MacBook Air or a Thunderbolt 4 MacBook Pro, that difference does not show up in day-to-day display or storage work. A Thunderbolt 5 dock connected to a Thunderbolt 4 host runs at Thunderbolt 4 speed. The TS5's extra bandwidth is only available on a Thunderbolt 5 host, such as an M5 Pro or M5 Max MacBook Pro.

The TS5 also supports three displays on a supported Thunderbolt 5 host and four on an M5 Max, per CalDigit. The TS4 is rated for dual displays. If you need a third screen, the TS4 is the wrong dock regardless of price.

### 2. 140W versus 98W charging

The TS5 supplies up to **140W** to the host, enough for a 16-inch MacBook Pro. The TS4 supplies up to **98W**.

A MacBook Air will not notice the difference; it draws far less. A 14-inch MacBook Pro may be fine at 98W in normal use but can benefit from the extra headroom under sustained load. A 16-inch M5 Pro or M5 Max is the clear case for 140W.

CalDigit claims both docks hold their advertised wattage even when other ports are busy, because the power supplies are sized for the whole dock. Treat that as a vendor claim, but the 230W versus 240W supply is the relevant hardware difference.

### 3. The TS4 has a DisplayPort output; the TS5 does not

This is the most practical difference for a two-monitor desk. The TS4 has a **DisplayPort 1.4** output plus two downstream Thunderbolt 4 ports. You can connect one monitor to DisplayPort and one to Thunderbolt, or two Thunderbolt/USB-C monitors to the downstream ports.

The TS5 has no DisplayPort or HDMI output. Monitors connect to its downstream Thunderbolt 5 ports, which is fine for USB-C and Thunderbolt displays but requires a USB-C to DisplayPort or HDMI adapter for older monitors.

If you own a DisplayPort monitor and want a clean single-cable desk, the TS4 is the simpler dock. If your monitors are USB-C or Thunderbolt, this difference disappears.

### 4. The TS4 has more USB ports

The TS4 has **eight USB ports**: three USB-C at 10Gb/s and five USB-A at 10Gb/s. The TS5 has **five**: three USB-C at 10Gb/s, one USB-A at 10Gb/s, and one USB-A at 480Mb/s for a keyboard or mouse.

If you have a keyboard, mouse, audio interface, card reader, and two USB drives, the TS4 covers them without a hub. The TS5 pushes you toward a USB-A hub or a Thunderbolt storage device. That is a real difference in daily use, not a spec-sheet footnote.

### 5. Price at launch was almost identical

At launch, the TS4 was **$359.95** in 2022 and the TS5 was **$369.99** in 2025. The newer dock was only $10 more at launch, which makes the TS5 look like the obvious upgrade. That is the wrong conclusion for a Thunderbolt 4 Mac with a DisplayPort monitor and a lot of USB-A gear: you would be paying for bandwidth and charging you cannot use, while giving up the ports you can.

Current prices are not launch prices. Check both vendor links before deciding; the older TS4 may be discounted, or it may be harder to find. We do not mirror live prices because they change and because a stale price is worse than a link.

In short: both docks can run two 6K60 displays from a single cable on the Air, but the TS4 gives you a dedicated DisplayPort output, and the TS5 gives you newer Thunderbolt ports you cannot fully use until you buy a newer Mac.

## Is Thunderbolt 5 worth it?

It depends on which of these you will actually use:

- **Triple or four displays.** Only the TS5 can do it, and only on a Thunderbolt 5 host. If you need three screens, this alone decides it.
- **80Gb/s storage.** A Thunderbolt 5 SSD can use the extra bandwidth; a Thunderbolt 4 SSD cannot. If your drives are Thunderbolt 4 or USB, the TS5's link is headroom, not speed.
- **140W charging.** A 16-inch MacBook Pro benefits; an Air does not.
- **Future laptop.** A TS5 bought today can move to a Thunderbolt 5 Mac later. A TS4 cannot become a Thunderbolt 5 dock.

If none of those apply, the TS4's DisplayPort output and extra USB ports are more useful today. "Future-proof" is only worth paying for when the future is on your upgrade plan.

## FAQ

### Is the TS5 worth it for a Thunderbolt 4 Mac?

Only if you need 140W charging or plan to buy a Thunderbolt 5 Mac. Otherwise, the TS4's DisplayPort output and extra USB ports are a better fit, and both run at the same 40Gb/s on a Thunderbolt 4 host.

### Can the TS4 run three displays?

CalDigit rates the TS4 for dual displays (or a single 8K). It does not advertise a triple-display mode. If you need three screens, the TS5 on a Thunderbolt 5 host is the supported path.

### Does the TS4 charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro at full speed?

It supplies up to 98W. A 16-inch MacBook Pro can draw more than that under heavy load, so the TS5's 140W is the safer choice for that laptop. For a MacBook Air or a 14-inch Pro, 98W is generally sufficient.

### Is the TS4 discontinued?

CalDigit still has a TS4 product page, but availability varies by region and retailer. Check the vendor link before assuming you can buy one.

### Which has more ports?

The TS4 has 18 ports by CalDigit's count, including eight USB ports and a DisplayPort output. The TS5 has 15, including four Thunderbolt 5 ports and five USB ports. More ports is not automatically better; it depends on which connectors your devices use.
