The Plugable TBT-UDH2 and CalDigit TS5 are both Thunderbolt 5 docks with 140W host charging, 2.5GbE, and a similar price. They solve the same problem in opposite ways.

The **Plugable** spends its ports on **two HDMI 2.1 outputs**, five USB-C ports, three USB-A ports, and a single 30W downstream Thunderbolt 5 port. The **CalDigit TS5** has **no HDMI or DisplayPort output at all**, but gives you four Thunderbolt 5 ports — one host and three downstream — plus five USB ports.

That makes the choice unusually clear once you know your monitors and your storage. This guide maps it out. We did not test either dock; all specs come from the vendors' own pages, checked on 8 November 2026.

## Quick answer

- **Buy the Plugable TBT-UDH2** if you have two HDMI monitors and want one cable without adapters, or if you need a lot of USB-C and USB-A ports.
- **Buy the CalDigit TS5** if your monitors are USB-C or Thunderbolt, you need three downstream Thunderbolt 5 ports for SSDs or displays, or you want four displays on an M5 Max.
- **On an M5 Air**, both docks can run two external displays; the Plugable is simpler for HDMI monitors, while the CalDigit needs adapters for them.

## What they share

- **140W host charging**, enough for a 16-inch MacBook Pro.
- **Thunderbolt 5 at 80Gb/s**, with 120Gb/s Bandwidth Boost for video.
- **2.5GbE Ethernet** and **SD/microSD 4.0** readers.
- **Dual-display support on a Thunderbolt 4 host**, including the M5 Air.
- **A single-cable desk**: one Thunderbolt cable carries displays, data, Ethernet, and power.

If your desk is two HDMI monitors, a keyboard, and a mouse, both docks will work. The decision comes down to the next section.

## The four differences that matter

### 1. Two HDMI outputs versus none

This is the headline. The Plugable has **two HDMI 2.1 ports**, each rated up to 8K at 60Hz or 4K at 144Hz. You can connect two HDMI monitors directly and keep the downstream Thunderbolt port for a third display on an M5 Pro or M5 Max. Those are vendor ceilings: dual 8K60 or 4K144 still depends on the host, the monitors, and Display Stream Compression support.

The CalDigit TS5 has no HDMI or DisplayPort output. Monitors connect to its Thunderbolt 5 ports, which is clean for USB-C and Thunderbolt displays but means a USB-C to HDMI or DisplayPort adapter for older monitors. If you own two HDMI monitors, the Plugable removes two adapters and two potential failure points.

### 2. One downstream Thunderbolt port versus three

The Plugable has **one downstream Thunderbolt 5 port**, rated at 30W. It is enough for a third display on an M5 Pro or Max, or for one fast Thunderbolt SSD.

The CalDigit has **three downstream Thunderbolt 5 ports**, each at 15W. That is the better dock for a Thunderbolt-heavy desk: multiple TB5 SSDs, a RAID array, or a daisy chain of Thunderbolt displays. CalDigit also supports triple displays on a Thunderbolt 5 host and four on an M5 Max, using those ports and a daisy chain.

If your peripherals are USB and HDMI, the Plugable's layout is more useful. If they are Thunderbolt, the CalDigit's is.

### 3. USB port count and front power

The Plugable has **five USB-C and three USB-A ports**, plus the downstream Thunderbolt 5 port. Two of its front ports are rated at 30W: one USB-C and the Thunderbolt 5 port. That is a lot of front-accessible charging for phones, tablets, and SSDs.

The CalDigit has **five USB ports**: three USB-C at 10Gb/s, one USB-A at 10Gb/s, and one USB-A at 480Mb/s for a keyboard or mouse. Its front USB-C port is 20W, and the downstream Thunderbolt ports are 15W each.

If you charge an iPad or a phone from the dock, the Plugable's 30W front ports are more convenient. If you need three Thunderbolt devices, the CalDigit wins.

### 4. Host compatibility

The Plugable lists compatibility with **Thunderbolt 5, Thunderbolt 4, and USB4** laptops. The CalDigit TS5 is broader: **Thunderbolt 5, 4, 3, USB4, USB4 v2, and USB-C hosts**, with reduced functionality on USB-C.

Every current Apple silicon Mac has Thunderbolt, so this mostly matters for older Intel Macs, iPads, or a mixed Windows desk. If you have a Thunderbolt 3 Mac, the CalDigit is the safer choice on paper. If you have a Thunderbolt 4 or 5 Mac, both are supported.

## Which Mac gets which

| Mac | Max external displays | Plugable TBT-UDH2 | CalDigit TS5 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MacBook Air (M5, Thunderbolt 4) | Two | Dual HDMI, no adapters | Dual via TB5 ports, adapters for HDMI |
| MacBook Pro (M5 Pro, Thunderbolt 5) | Three, per CalDigit | Two HDMI + one via downstream TB5 | Three via downstream TB5 ports |
| MacBook Pro (M5 Max, Thunderbolt 5) | Four, per CalDigit | Plugable advertises a third display, not four | Four with a daisy-chained display |

The Air is the simple case: both docks can drive two 6K60 displays, and the Plugable is easier if those monitors are HDMI. The [M5 Air dock guide](/articles/best-dock-for-macbook-air-m5-dual-monitors/) covers the cable and DSC details.

The M5 Max is where the CalDigit pulls ahead. Four displays need more than two HDMI outputs plus one Thunderbolt port; CalDigit's three downstream Thunderbolt ports and daisy-chain support are built for that. Plugable's page advertises a third display, not a fourth, so do not buy it expecting a four-screen desk.

## Price and value

At the time of writing, Plugable lists the TBT-UDH2 at **$349.95**. The CalDigit TS5 launched at **$369.99** in 2025. Those are close enough that price should not be the deciding factor; the port layout should.

What you pay for with the Plugable: two HDMI 2.1 outputs, five USB-C ports, three USB-A ports, and two 30W front ports.
What you pay for with the CalDigit: three downstream Thunderbolt 5 ports, a broader host list, and four-display support on an M5 Max.

Neither is "better" in the abstract. A two-HDMI-monitor desk is paying for Thunderbolt ports it may not use with the CalDigit, and a Thunderbolt-SSD desk is paying for HDMI ports it may not use with the Plugable.

## FAQ

### Which dock is better for two HDMI monitors?

The Plugable TBT-UDH2. It has two HDMI 2.1 outputs, so you do not need adapters. The CalDigit TS5 can drive two HDMI monitors only through USB-C to HDMI adapters on its Thunderbolt ports.

### Can the CalDigit TS5 run four displays?

CalDigit says yes on an M5 Max, with one display daisy-chained. The exact cabling depends on your monitors; check CalDigit's display notes before buying.

### Can the Plugable run four displays?

Plugable advertises a third display via the downstream Thunderbolt 5 port on M5 Pro and M5 Max machines. It does not claim four. If you need four, the CalDigit is the supported path.

### Do both charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro at 140W?

Both advertise 140W host charging. That is enough for a 16-inch MacBook Pro, subject to the laptop's own charging behaviour and the dock's power supply.

### Which has more USB ports?

The Plugable has five USB-C and three USB-A ports, plus its downstream Thunderbolt 5 port. The CalDigit has five USB ports total. If USB peripherals are the priority, the Plugable's layout is more generous.
