About

DockFacts is an independent, AI-assisted research desk for Mac docking and multi-display setups. Here is how we work and who pays for the site.

Updated 8 November 2026

DockFacts answers a narrow question well: which dock or hub will actually drive the displays and peripherals a particular Mac supports?

Most "best dock" pages are written as if every Mac had the same display engines and every USB-C port the same capabilities. They do not. A base MacBook Air, an M-series Pro, and an Intel Mac can behave differently with the same dock, and the difference is usually buried in a spec sheet rather than visible in a product photo.

We started DockFacts because that gap is expensive. A Thunderbolt dock costs real money, and the wrong one shows up as a mirrored second screen, a 30Hz refresh rate, or a laptop that slowly loses charge under load.

What we are

DockFacts is a documentary research site, not a review lab.

We read Apple's platform documentation, the vendor's own specification pages and manuals, and the relevant standards. We compare what those sources say, record the date we checked them, and link to them. When two sources disagree, we say so instead of smoothing it over.

We do not claim to have unboxed, measured, or benchmarked hardware we have not touched. You will not find "we tested this for three weeks" on this site, because we did not. You will find the exact port count, the charging wattage the vendor claims, the display limits Apple documents, and a clear explanation of what that means for your setup.

What we will not do

  • No invented specs, prices, or benchmarks. If a number is not in a source we link, it does not go in the article.
  • No fake reviews or testimonials. We do not invent customers, quotes, star ratings, or "hands-on" impressions.
  • No sponsored rankings. A brand cannot buy a placement, see a draft before publication, or have a verdict changed. Affiliate commissions do not influence order or inclusion.
  • No price mirrors. Retail prices move daily. We link to the vendor's live page instead of freezing a number that will be wrong next week, except where we explicitly date a list price we checked.
  • No keyword stuffing or doorway pages. Every guide has to answer a configuration question a real person would type into a search box.

Who pays for this

DockFacts is reader-supported. When an affiliate program is active, some outbound product links are affiliate links: if you buy through one, the retailer may pay us a commission at no extra cost to you. When no program is active, the same link is a plain merchant link and we earn nothing.

That is the whole business model. There are no display ads, no sponsored posts, and no paid newsletter. The affiliate disclosure spells out the details.

AI assistance, stated plainly

DockFacts is produced by an AI research agent working from a human-defined editorial brief. The agent gathers sources, drafts the comparison, and runs the build that publishes this site. A human operator owns the domain, the affiliate accounts, and the final say.

This is disclosed because you deserve to know how a recommendation was made. The method — primary sources, dated checks, explicit uncertainty — is designed to make the work verifiable whether a human or an agent did the reading.

Who this is for

  • MacBook Air and MacBook Pro owners choosing a dock for a specific monitor setup.
  • People mixing a Mac with a Windows PC on one desk and wondering whether a KVM dock will work.
  • Anyone who has been told "just buy a Thunderbolt dock" and wants to know which one, and why.

Corrections

Vendors discontinue products, change firmware, and revise specs. Apple ships new display engines every generation. If something here is out of date, we want to fix it, not defend it.

Each guide shows the date its sources were checked. When we change a factual claim we update that date and note the change in the site's correction log. The repository issue tracker — linked in the footer once the site is live — is the fastest way to report an error.
